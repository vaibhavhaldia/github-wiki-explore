Here is the full English translation and synthesis of the Chinese document — **"Programmer's Guide to Living Longer"** (程序员延寿指南) — rendered as an evidence dashboard above.

**Key terminology:** The entire document is organized around **ACM — All-Cause Mortality**, the probability of dying from any cause within a study window. Reducing ACM is the proxy for living longer.

**The analytical framework** the document uses is honest about its limits:

The relationship between ACM reduction and lifespan extension is **non-linear** (modeled as `ΔLifeSpan = (1/(1 + ΔACM) − 1) × 10`). Variables are **not independently additive** — you cannot simply sum up "−23% from chili + −47% from racket sports = −70%." And all cited evidence expresses **correlation, not causation** — a classic example given: people who walk 7,000+ steps daily have lower ACM, but that population also has fewer long-term illness sufferers, which may explain the effect.

**Top-line takeaways by category:**

*Highest-ROI actions* — racket sports 3×/week (−47% ACM), nuts daily (−27%), vegetables and fruit (−26%), sunlight (−40%), brushing teeth (−25%).

*Biggest hazards to eliminate first* — smoking (+50% ACM, −11 to 12 years), heavy alcohol (+50%), ultra-processed food (up to +62%), sugary drinks.

*Sleep* — 7 hours nightly is the global optimum. Both under- and over-sleeping increase mortality. Bedtime timing findings are marked disputed in the original.

*Supplements* — spermidine (naturally found in wheat germ, natto, broccoli) and glucosamine show strong signals (−30–60% and −39% respectively), though effect sizes should be interpreted cautiously. Metformin data applies specifically to diabetics.

*Context factors* — pessimism is a measurable mortality risk; optimism alone is not protective. Wealth is independently correlated with longevity even when controlling for genetics.
